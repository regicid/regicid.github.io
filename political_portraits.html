    Our main tool: BERT

    Most steps of the pipeline rely on BERT-type models, here CamemBERT, a French BERT developed at Inria. Unlike LLMs, BERT does not write text: it reads a passage and turns each word into a representation of its meaning in context. A small layer added on top turns that representation into a label: a section, a person’s name, a judgement. Because BERT captures how the language works, the same base model serves many downstream tasks: classification, topic modelling, matching, named-entity recognition, sentiment analysis.

    It is ideal for our purposes: open, frugal, fast, elegant and highly reliable. BERT models are small (CamemBERT-large has about 335 million parameters, a tiny fraction of an LLM), so they run fast on a single graphics card. They are open: anyone can download them, inspect them and rerun our analyses. And once fine-tuned on a precise task, they classify almost as well as much larger models.

    La plupart des étapes reposent sur des modèles de type BERT, ici CamemBERT, un BERT français développé à l’Inria. Contrairement aux LLM, BERT n’écrit pas de texte : il lit un passage et transforme chaque mot en une représentation de son sens en contexte. Une petite couche ajoutée par-dessus transforme cette représentation en étiquette : une rubrique, un nom de personne, un jugement. Parce qu’il saisit le fonctionnement de la langue, un même modèle BERT sert à de nombreuses tâches en aval : classification, modélisation thématique, appariement, reconnaissance d’entités nommées, analyse de sentiments.

    C’est parfait pour notre usage : ouvert, parcimonieux, rapide, élégant et très fiable. Les modèles BERT sont petits (CamemBERT-large compte environ 335 millions de paramètres, une infime fraction d’un LLM) et tournent vite sur une seule carte graphique. Ils sont ouverts : chacun peut les télécharger, les examiner et refaire nos analyses. Et une fois affinés sur une tâche précise, ils classent presque aussi bien que des modèles bien plus gros.

    From article to portrait

    An article is classified into a rubrique by a fine-tuned CamemBERT, its quotes are extracted, and its mentions are found by a fine-tuned CamemBERT NER. Coreference with qwen-propp, a fine-tuned Qwen, links mentions into chains; assemble turns each chain into a character holding its mentions and attached words; gender is then inferred; quotes are linked to their speaker; a fine-tuned CamemBERT scores each mention on valence, extremism and momentum.Un article est classé dans une rubrique par un CamemBERT affiné, ses citations sont extraites et ses mentions repérées par un CamemBERT affiné. La coréférence avec qwen-propp, un Qwen affiné, relie les mentions en chaînes ; assemble fait de chaque chaîne un personnage avec ses mentions et les mots qui s'y rattachent ; le genre est ensuite inféré ; les citations sont reliées à leur locuteur ; un CamemBERT affiné note chaque mention en valence, extrémisme et dynamique.

    Computing the scores

    Le Monde, 1944 to November 2026 (3.2 million articles, every section, 389,000 of them in politics); and 32 French news sites, 2008 to November 2026 (1.48 million politics articles; more sections are being added). The « Sections » filter sets which sections count, and the newspapers offered are those that have them; the page opens on politics. The section comes from n-gramsci/rubriques-presse, and from the same classifier after June 2026 and for the 0.5% of Le Monde articles it lacks. People are found by named-entity recognition and Qwen coreference. Each passage centred on a mention is scored by portrayal-camembert-large v2 (seed 0), trained on codebook v4.

    Valence is P(positive) − P(negative) judgement of the person. Extremism is P(framed as radical or extreme) − P(framed as moderate). Momentum is P(rising in the race) − P(declining). All three run from −1 to +1. Most passages carry no judgement, so averages sit close to 0.

    A point averages every scored passage, weighted by the probability that the passage says something about the person. Long portraits weigh more than passing mentions. Shaded bands and whiskers are 95% intervals, with articles as the resampling unit.

    A person is named by the full name in their coreference chain. A chain that only says « Mélenchon » or « M. Mélenchon » takes the article’s own « Jean-Luc Mélenchon » when there is exactly one; otherwise the person that family name stands for that year, when one person holds at least 90% of the articles naming someone « … Mélenchon ». Le Pen, place names, and family names that are also first names (Philippe, Bertrand) are never resolved that way. A name groups every spelling that differs only by case, accents or hyphens, and homonyms are merged. The search suggests names found in at least 30 articles; any other name can be looked up directly. Scores are read live from the Ngramsci API, month by month and newspaper by newspaper.

    How the models are trained

    Claude Sonnet annotated 12,410 press passages (2008–2026) following the codebook below. We then fine-tuned a small model, CamemBERT-large, on these annotations, so that it can score millions of mentions.

    Think of it as distillation. A large, expensive model reads a sample of the press carefully, like an expert, and a small model learns to reproduce its judgements. The small model is less subtle passage by passage, but it can read the whole corpus, which would be far too slow and costly for the large one, and its averages stay very close to what the expert would have said.

    The score of a single mention is noisy: the model agrees with the annotator at κ ≈ 0.7, about as often as Claude Opus agrees with Claude Sonnet. Errors are mostly random and cancel out. With a few hundred mentions per person and period, the model’s averages match the annotator’s closely (r ≈ 0.96–0.98). Gaps between two people below about 0.06 in valence (0.03 for extremism, 0.04 for momentum) are within the model’s error. Trends for one person are measured more reliably than gaps between people.

    Claude Sonnet a annoté 12 410 passages de presse (2008–2026) en suivant le codebook ci-dessous. Sur ces annotations, nous avons affiné un petit modèle, CamemBERT-large, capable de noter des millions de mentions.

    On peut y voir une distillation. Un grand modèle, coûteux, lit attentivement un échantillon de presse, comme un expert, et un petit modèle apprend à reproduire ses jugements. Le petit modèle est moins fin passage par passage, mais il peut lire tout le corpus, ce qui serait bien trop lent et coûteux pour le grand, et ses moyennes restent très proches de ce qu’aurait dit l’expert.

    La note d’une mention isolée est bruitée : le modèle s’accorde avec l’annotateur à κ ≈ 0,7, à peu près autant que Claude Opus avec Claude Sonnet. Les erreurs sont surtout aléatoires et se compensent. Avec quelques centaines de mentions par personne et par période, les moyennes du modèle suivent de près celles de l’annotateur (r ≈ 0,96–0,98). Un écart entre deux personnes inférieur à environ 0,06 en valence (0,03 en extrémisme, 0,04 en dynamique) reste dans la marge d’erreur du modèle. Les évolutions d’une même personne sont mieux mesurées que les écarts entre personnes.

    You are annotating French press excerpts about French politics (2008–2027).
    Each line of the input file is a JSON object with: "wid", "full_name" (the TARGET), "title" (the article's title), "passage". In the passage, the TARGET's name is written between [[ ]]; every [[ ]] refers to the same name.
    - TITLE: the article's title, for context only. Do not judge it.
    - PASSAGE: the sentence where the TARGET is named, plus the neighbouring sentences attached to this mention. This is what you judge.
    SCOPE
    Judge how the PASSAGE portrays the TARGET at this point of the article: the sentence naming the TARGET and any neighbouring sentence that directly continues it (a comment, an apposition, a reaction, a parenthesis such as « (condamnée en appel…) », a label such as « la candidate d'extrême droite »). Only judgements about the TARGET count: a judgement about another person, even in the same sentence, does not count.
    For each line, output one JSON object with these fields:
    - "wid": copied from the input.
    - "designe": 1 if the mention designates the politician named in full_name, even through a phrase that contains another name (« la fille de Jean-Marie [[Le Pen]] » designates Marine Le Pen → 1); 0 if it designates someone or something else (a namesake, a relative, a place, a company, a street, e.g. « le proviseur [[Philippe]] Tamisier », « sa sœur Marie-Caroline [[Le Pen]] »). If 0, set every other field to null. If 1, fill every field, even when "pertinent" is 0 (the other fields are then usually 0); never use null when designe is 1.
    - "pertinent": 1 if the passage says something about the TARGET (an action, a statement, a situation, a judgement); 0 if the TARGET is only mentioned in passing (a list of names, a mere comparison, the passage is about something else).
    - "valence": how the TARGET is judged.
    -1 negative: criticism, failure, scandal, legal trouble, ridicule, mockery, an attack on the TARGET, an unflattering description, including when it is implicit or sarcastic (« perfectionne le racolage… », « à quoi joue [[X]] ? »).
    0 neutral or factual, or no judgement. A plain agenda fact is 0.
    1 positive: praise, success, competence, popularity, sympathy (« Et ça marche ! », « c'est un bon candidat »). Do not use valence for polls (→ "dynamique") or for labels such as « extrême » or « radical » (→ "extremisme"). A passage can be valence -1 AND extremisme 1. If the TARGET attacks or criticises someone else, that is not a negative judgement of the TARGET. Reported criticism: if the author reports a criticism of the TARGET in order to dispute, denounce or mock it (« cette calomnie », « comme si c'était établi »), valence follows the author (0, or 1 if the author defends the TARGET). A criticism reported without distance (« X est accusé de… », « Y dénonce X ») is -1. Mixed passages: give the dominant judgement; if they are truly balanced, 0.
    - "extremisme": how radical or moderate the TARGET is framed, on a -1 / 0 / 1 scale.
    1 extreme: the TARGET or their positions are framed as radical or extreme, either with an explicit label (extrême droite / gauche, ultragauche, radical, populiste, nationaliste, révolutionnaire, hors de l'« arc républicain », factieux…) or in substance: their positions or project are presented as dangerous, unrealistic, frightening, a threat to the economy, institutions or democracy (« allié et soutien de Poutine », « complaisance pour les dictatures »).
    -1 moderate: the TARGET is framed as moderate, centrist, reasonable, reassuring, consensual, statesmanlike, within the « arc républicain », or as having become acceptable or mainstream (« modéré », « raisonnable », « rassurant », « présidentiable », « respectable »).
    0 no framing on this dimension (the most frequent case). Rules:
    - As for valence, a framing by others reported without distance counts: « la gauche accuse [[X]] de flirter avec l'extrême droite » → 1.
    - A framing of the TARGET's party or movement counts when the passage ties the TARGET to it (the TARGET leads it, defends it, embodies it in the passage).
    - The TARGET's own claims do not count unless the passage endorses them: « il se veut rassurant » alone is 0; a claim the author undercuts (« le candidat des modérés qu'il prétend être ») is not -1.
    - The TARGET's own slogans (« révolution citoyenne ») and non-political uses (« les deux extrêmes » of a range of pension options) do not count.
    - A strategy reported as such is not a framing: « sa stratégie de dédiabolisation » is 0; « une candidate désormais respectable » is -1.
    - If both poles appear, follow what the passage concludes (« malgré sa dédiabolisation, toujours d'extrême droite » → 1).
    - Ordinary criticism (« manque d'expérience ») or ordinary praise (« compétent ») is 0 here: it is valence.
    - "dynamique": the TARGET's trajectory in the race.
    1 rising: gaining in polls, new support or rallies, a breakthrough, momentum.
    -1 declining: falling in polls, losing support, isolated, pressed to withdraw.
    0 no information. A poll score with no movement and no comparison is 0, even if it is low (« crédité de 2 % », « ferme la marche »).
    - A passage may start with "[Note: this passage is part of a quotation that begins earlier in the article.]": the words are then a quoted person's, not the author's, even if no quotation mark is visible in the passage.
    - "journaliste": 1 if the passage's judgement of the TARGET (a non-zero valence or extremisme) is the author's own; 0 if it is carried by someone quoted or reported (another person, or the TARGET about themself), or if there is no judgement.
    - "note": at most 8 words (French is fine) naming the decisive cue, e.g. « condamnée en appel (apposition) ».
    Write one JSON object per input line, in the same order, one per line, to the output file. Output only valid JSON lines.

    Code and models

    Everything is open: the code and every model below can be downloaded and reused.

    • ngramsci_nlp: the whole pipeline, from raw articles to scored mentions (MIT licence).
    • bert-rubriques: classifies each article into a section; the sections of the existing corpus are in n-gramsci/rubriques-presse.
    • propp-fr NER (on CamemBERT-large): finds the mentions of people; propp-fr assembles them into characters.
    • Qwen3.5-4B coreference: links « il », « le président », « Macron » into one chain per person.
    • GenderedNews REBEL (on BARThez): extracts quotes and their speakers.
    • portrayal-camembert-large: scores each mention for valence, extremism and momentum.

    Tout est ouvert : le code et chacun des modèles ci-dessous sont téléchargeables et réutilisables.

    • ngramsci_nlp : toute la chaîne, des articles bruts aux mentions notées (licence MIT).
    • bert-rubriques : classe chaque article dans une rubrique ; les rubriques du corpus existant sont dans n-gramsci/rubriques-presse.
    • propp-fr NER (sur CamemBERT-large) : repère les mentions de personnes ; propp-fr les assemble en personnages.
    • Qwen3.5-4B coréférence : relie « il », « le président », « Macron » en une chaîne par personne.
    • REBEL de GenderedNews (sur BARThez) : extrait les citations et leurs locuteurs.
    • portrayal-camembert-large : note chaque mention en valence, extrémisme et dynamique.
